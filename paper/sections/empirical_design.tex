\section{Proposed Empirical Design}
\label{sec:design}

This section presents the specifications, defines the treatment, describes the data, and pairs each identifying assumption with its threat and the specification that would probe it.

\subsection{Specification}

The baseline continuous-treatment difference-in-differences, estimated on the North African sample alone, is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:baseline}
\ln M_{pct} = \beta \, (\tau_p \times C_{st}) + \phi_{pc} + \psi_{ct} + u_{pct},
\end{equation}
where $M_{pct}$ is the import value (EUR) of product $p$ from partner $c$ in year $t$; $\tau_p$ is product-level carbon-cost exposure; $C_{st}$ is the effective carbon cost of the supplying sector $s = s(p)$; $\phi_{pc}$ are product-by-partner fixed effects; and $\psi_{ct}$ are partner-by-year fixed effects. The preferred triple-difference specification pools the corridor with a comparison partner group:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:triple}
\ln M_{pct} = \beta \, (\tau_p \times C_{st} \times L_c) + \gamma \, (C_{st} \times L_c) + \phi_{pc} + \psi_{ct} + \eta_{pt} + u_{pct},
\end{equation}
where $L_c$ indicates the lower-regulation corridor (Morocco, Algeria, Egypt, Tunisia) and $\eta_{pt}$ are product-by-year fixed effects. A parallel specification would replace $\ln M_{pct}$ with quantity to separate volume from unit-price movements.

\subsection{Variable Definitions}

\paragraph{Carbon-cost exposure ($\tau_p$).}
The CO$_2$ emission intensity of the sector supplying product $p$ under the European industrial activity classification NACE Rev.~2, mapped to products through the official Eurostat correspondence between the Combined Nomenclature (CN) and NACE \citep{ramon}, fixed at its 2005--2007 average to prevent endogenous intensity responses. Units matter for interpretation. Measuring $\tau_p$ in tonnes of CO$_2$ per euro of gross output and $C_{st}$ in euro per tonne makes the product $\tau_p \times C_{st}$ the carbon cost per euro of output, a dimensionless share, so $\beta$ reads as the percentage change in imports per percentage-point rise in that share. The transparent alternative is a binary CBAM Annex~I indicator \citep{cbam2023}.

\paragraph{Effective carbon cost ($C_{st}$).}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cst}
C_{st} = P_t \times (1 - a_{st}),
\end{equation}
where $P_t$ is the annual average EUA price \citep{eeaprice} and $a_{st}$ is the sector-year free-allocation share from the EU Transaction Log \citep{eutl}. Free-allocation attenuation is part of the treatment definition: a sector with 100 per cent free allocation faces zero effective cost regardless of the EUA price ($a_{st} \Uparrow \implies C_{st} \Downarrow$). An objection is that tradable allowances carry the full permit price as marginal opportunity cost, so lump-sum allocation would leave the output margin unaffected. EU allocation is not lump-sum in this strict sense: activity-level thresholds, closure provisions, and partial-cessation rules tie allowances to continued production, which gives free allocation genuine marginal bite \citep{far2011}. A second concern is mechanical endogeneity: contemporaneous emissions enter the denominator of the allocation share, so demand shocks that cut emissions would raise $a_{st}$ by construction. The baseline therefore computes $a_{st}$ relative to fixed 2005--2007 verified emissions. In Phases~I and~II, $a_{st}$ can exceed one, and the baseline caps it at one. The contemporaneous share and the uncapped version are retained as robustness checks.

\paragraph{Comparison group ($L_c = 0$).}
The choice of comparison group determines what $\beta$ identifies. Three candidates merit consideration. Turkey participates in an EU customs union covering industrial products \citep{customs1995}, so carbon-cost effects are hard to separate from preferential-access effects. It priced no carbon within the sample and operated only installation-level monitoring and reporting from 2015 \citep{icap2026}. The Western Balkans (Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia) share geographic proximity, operate under Stabilisation and Association Agreements with comparable tariff schedules, and lacked carbon-pricing regimes throughout the sample period \citep{worldbank2026}, a cleaner regulatory comparison. Their limitation is smaller export volumes in emission-intensive goods, which introduces noise. An intra-EU sourcing benchmark would control for EU consumption shocks but faces EU~ETS regulation by construction, so it is not a lower-regulation comparator. The design would report results for each group.

\paragraph{Fixed-effect absorption.}
In Equation~\ref{eq:triple}, $\eta_{pt}$ absorbs $\tau_p \times C_{st}$ and $\phi_{pc}$ absorbs $\tau_p \times L_c$. The third two-way term is not absorbed. Since $C_{st}$ varies across sectors within a partner-year cell, which $\psi_{ct}$ holds fixed, $C_{st} \times L_c$ survives the fixed effects and must enter explicitly as $\gamma$ (cf.\ Figure~\ref{fig:absorption}). Both $\tau_p$ and $C_{st}$ are defined at sector level, so treatment varies by sector, year, and corridor status, and the CN8 disaggregation constructs the outcome and permits $\eta_{pt}$ rather than adding treatment variation. Because $\tau_p$ is time-invariant, $\beta$ is separated from $\gamma$ only under the assumption that the corridor's differential response is linear in emission intensity.

\subsection{Sample and Data}

The sample covers 2005 to 2021, spanning Phase~I through the first year of Phase~IV. Pre-2005 years (2000--2004) are reserved for pre-trend validation.\footnote{The Directive dates from 2003, so 2000 to 2002 constitutes a clean pre-announcement period and 2003 to 2004 a potential anticipation window.} The window ends in 2021 by design: the 2022 energy-price shock, driven by the Russian invasion of Ukraine, distorted both EUA prices and bilateral trade flows in ways unrelated to the leakage channel.

Bilateral trade data at the CN8 level would come from Eurostat Comext (dataset DS-045409; \citealp{comext}), which reports value and quantity by reporting Member State, partner country, product code and year. UN Comtrade data at the six-digit Harmonised System level (HS6) would be the fallback for missing partner-product cells \citep{comtrade}. The coarser classification reduces product-level variation but has wider country coverage. Sectoral CO$_2$ emission intensities would be computed from Eurostat air emissions accounts, defined as tonnes of CO$_2$ per unit of gross output in the corresponding NACE Rev.~2 sector, and mapped to trade products through the Eurostat CN-to-NACE correspondence \citep{ramon}.

\subsection{Identifying Assumptions and Threats}

\begin{enumerate}

\item \emph{Parallel trends.} Absent the EU~ETS, differential trends in high-exposure imports from the corridor (relative to comparison partners) would match those for low-exposure products. An event-study specification interacting $\tau_p \times L_c$ with year indicators (base 2004) would inspect 2000--2002 for pre-trends and 2003--2004 for anticipation.

\item \emph{No confounding product shocks.} Global shocks correlated with emission intensity do not differentially affect the corridor versus comparison partners. $\eta_{pt}$ absorbs all product-by-year variation. A remaining violation would require a shock simultaneously product-specific, intensity-correlated, and corridor-differential.

\item \emph{Carbon-price exogeneity.} The EUA price, set by EU-wide policy and macro factors, is plausibly exogenous to individual bilateral flows. $\psi_{ct}$ absorbs partner-year variation, so identification comes from within-partner-year, across-sector variation.

\item \emph{No coincident trade-policy changes.} Euro-Mediterranean association agreement tariff phase-outs could confound the estimate if they coincided with EU~ETS phases and disproportionately affected emission-intensive goods. The agreements entered into force at different dates (Tunisia 1998, Morocco 2000, Egypt 2004, Algeria 2005; \citealp{eumed}), and industrial tariff phase-outs were largely completed before the post-2018 price acceleration. The triple-difference mitigates this insofar as comparison partners face similar preferential arrangements.

\item \emph{Over-allocation attenuation.} $a_{st}$ exceeding one in Phases~I and~II mechanically weakens the treatment. Capping $a_{st}$ at one sets $C_{st} = 0$ for over-allocated sector-years. The uncapped version is retained as a robustness check.

\item \emph{CBAM anticipation.} The July 2021 proposal could alter 2021 trade patterns, but anticipation of a border charge deters leakage and biases $\beta$ toward zero. The event-study specification would detect any anomalous 2021 coefficient.

\item \emph{Pandemic disruption.} The years 2020 and 2021 coincide with severe trade and supply-chain disruption. $\psi_{ct}$ absorbs partner-specific pandemic severity and $\eta_{pt}$ absorbs product-specific demand collapses. A residual violation would require pandemic effects that were simultaneously corridor-specific and correlated with emission intensity. A robustness check would re-estimate the specification excluding 2020 and 2021.

\item \emph{No coincident trade-defence measures.} The EU imposed definitive safeguard measures on steel imports in February 2019, allocating tariff-rate quotas by product category and origin \citep{safeguard2019}. These bind on emission-intensive products, differentiate by partner, and fall inside the post-2018 window that supplies the strongest treatment, so they would attenuate $\beta$ for steel. Estimating the heterogeneity analysis by product group isolates the affected categories, and dropping steel is the sharper check.

\item \emph{Corridor-specific energy costs.} Algeria and Egypt produce natural gas and hold domestic energy prices below international benchmarks \citep{iea2026}, so the corridor's cost advantage in gas-intensive products widens when European gas prices rise. European gas prices in turn co-move with the EUA price, most strongly after 2018. This confound is corridor-specific, intensity-correlated, and time-varying, so no fixed effect in Equation~\ref{eq:triple} absorbs it. The specification would add a control interacting $\tau_p \times L_c$ with the EU--partner gas-price differential. Without this control, $\beta$ would bundle the carbon-cost asymmetry with the energy-cost asymmetry.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Estimation, Inference, and Implementation}

Under heterogeneous treatment effects, two-way fixed-effects estimators weight the underlying comparisons in ways that need not be convex, so the estimated coefficient can differ in sign from every unit-level effect \citep{dechaisemartin2020}. A continuous dose compounds this: identification requires a parallel-trends condition holding at every level of treatment intensity rather than only between treated and untreated units, and the resulting estimand is a weighted average of dose-specific responses whose weights depend on the distribution of doses \citep{callaway2024}. The design would therefore report the distribution of $\tau_p \times C_{st}$ across sector-years, estimate dose-specific responses in place of a single slope where cell counts permit, and apply current diagnostics for negative weighting \citep{baker2026}.

Standard errors would be clustered by sector, the level at which the treatment varies, since clustering on product would treat mechanically identical CN8 treatment values as independent \citep{cameron2011}. The partner dimension is the binding constraint: four corridor and six comparison partners give ten partner clusters, below the range in which cluster-robust asymptotics are reliable.\footnote{At this number the asymptotic variance understates true sampling variability, so a wild cluster bootstrap on partner clusters, rather than the cluster-robust estimator, would carry the inference.} The design would be implemented with ordinary least squares (OLS) and high-dimensional fixed effects (\texttt{fixest}; \citealp{berge2021}) and with Poisson pseudo-maximum-likelihood (PPML) estimation (\texttt{fepois}) to handle zero trade flows consistently \citep{santos2006}.

\subsection{Validation}

A timing placebo would assess specificity. Assigning the EUA price path a counterfactual four-year lead should collapse $\beta$, whereas a confound co-moving with the corridor would survive it. A placebo on low-intensity products would not discriminate, since the treatment is near zero there by construction. A heterogeneity analysis would estimate $\beta$ separately for the main CBAM product groups, whose trade exposure differs: cement's low value-to-weight ratio confines trade to short sea routes, which the Mediterranean corridor provides, whereas steel and fertilisers trade globally \citep{branger2016}. A positive $\beta$ would not by itself separate corridor-specific leakage from trade diversion, defined here as re-routing of existing output rather than new production. Leakage implies exports rise across all destinations, whereas diversion implies flat totals with a rising EU share. Corridor exports to non-EU destinations, available at HS6 from UN Comtrade \citep{comtrade}, would discriminate between them.

Interpretation needs a scale, not only a sign. The design would convert $\beta$ into the implied change in embodied carbon imported through the corridor and express it as a share of the EU~ETS emissions reduction estimated for regulated firms, so that a statistically precise estimate can still be judged economically small.

A positive and precisely estimated $\beta$ would be consistent with corridor-specific leakage. A precisely estimated zero would suggest that free allocation has been sufficient. An imprecise zero would leave the question unresolved and motivate more disaggregated data.

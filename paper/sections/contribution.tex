\section{Contribution and Use to Other Researchers}
\label{sec:contribution}

The proposed design would contribute along three dimensions. First, geographic targeting: existing trade-flow evidence does not interact carbon stringency with partner regulatory status, so corridor-specific leakage goes untested. By restricting the analysis to North Africa, the design isolates a corridor where regulatory asymmetry is large, transport costs are low, and production capacity in emission-intensive sectors already exists. A positive $\beta$ would indicate that the aggregate nulls documented by \citet{naegele2019} conceal heterogeneity, a finding directly relevant for calibrating CBAM border adjustments by partner country. It would also supply an empirical corridor-level benchmark for the leakage responses that ex-ante evaluations of the CBAM currently derive from calibrated simulation models \citep{bellora2023}. A well-identified null would carry a different but equally informative implication: that free allocation has been sufficient to prevent import leakage even where it is most likely.

Second, treatment measurement. \citet{naegele2019} already net free allocation out of their stringency measure, so the contribution is not the netting but two refinements to it. The allocation share is computed against a fixed 2005--2007 emissions denominator, which breaks the mechanical endogeneity that arises when demand shocks move contemporaneous emissions and with them the treatment. The netted treatment is then carried through the post-2018 window that a sample ending in 2011 cannot reach. This matters because sectors on the carbon-leakage list received 100 per cent of the benchmark allocation for free during most of the sample, so effective cost was near zero precisely where exposure was highest, and the identifying variation comes disproportionately from Phase~IV allocation cuts and the post-2018 price. Researchers estimating competitiveness, innovation, or emissions effects would benefit from the same two adjustments.

Third, temporal coverage: most existing studies end before 2015, leaving the post-2018 price acceleration untested for leakage.

The triple-difference structure is adaptable to other bilateral trade questions where regulation differentially affects product categories and partners. The comparison-group choice, the free-allocation treatment, and the absorption logic are transferable to studies of CBAM effectiveness once the definitive regime generates post-treatment variation.

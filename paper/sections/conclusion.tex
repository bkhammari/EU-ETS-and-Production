\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper has proposed a triple-difference design to estimate whether increases in the effective EU~ETS carbon cost raised EU imports of emission-intensive goods from North African partners over 2005 to 2021. Product-by-year fixed effects would absorb global product-specific shocks; partner-by-year fixed effects would absorb macroeconomic and exchange-rate confounds. The triple interaction would be identified from the differential response of high-exposure products sourced from the lower-regulation corridor.

The design has limitations. Import flows capture the trade channel of leakage but not physical relocation; a positive estimate would document increased sourcing without establishing that EU production was displaced. The North African corridor accounts for a small share of total EU imports of emission-intensive goods, so external validity to larger partners is uncertain. Partner-side production data, which would test whether increased exports correspond to expanded capacity rather than trade diversion from third markets, are unavailable at the required disaggregation for all four countries.

A concrete next step would be to assemble the Comext panel with free-allocation shares from the EU Transaction Log and sectoral emission intensities from Eurostat, and estimate the specification. The CBAM definitive regime took effect in January 2026; an extension of the panel beyond 2023, using transitional-phase reports, would allow estimating whether the border adjustment altered the pre-CBAM pattern.
